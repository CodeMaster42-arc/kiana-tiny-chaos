# Bedroom Adventures: level-design brief

Design inference: teach one idea, give a readable goal and immediate retry, then combine learned mechanics. Avoid time pressure while assessing the core physics controls. Prototype solution tests demonstrate a route, not fun, ideal difficulty, or player retention.

1. A little hello: single-target drop; learn aiming without distraction.
2. Around the bend: two targets at different heights; discover rolling on ramps.
3. The domino effect: wider board of linked toys; see indirect contacts.
4. Spring into it: spring pads and three routes; learn rebound direction.
5. A little more chaos: first combined checkpoint; optional mastery through fewer drops.
6. Two little detours: choose between side routes and central gap.
7. Domino duet: spacing and cascading contacts.
8. Bounce between friends: rebound timing with an open centre route.
9. A balancing act: off-centre contact with a pivoted board.
10. Bedroom finale: recombine familiar pieces rather than introduce a surprise rule.

All levels allow three drops; one/two/three drops earn three/two/one stars. Saved friends remain freed for the current attempt. Level selection stays unrestricted during alpha playtesting. There are no energy timers, premium boosters or pay-to-progress gates.

Physics validation: exact shape-to-bubble overlap, activated collision chains (a settling prop cannot rescue a friend on its own), fixed 120Hz simulation, reproducible test routes. Future playtests should record tutorial comprehension, missed inputs, retry frustration and which routes players discover. Do not infer ideal difficulty from simulation alone.

Research: Rovio's discussion of tactile physics in Dream Blast (https://www.rovio.com/articles/machine-learning-meets-puzzle-game-design/); stable structure research (https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02614); adaptive difficulty research (https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02518). These inform the process, not a claim that these ten levels are validated commercially.
